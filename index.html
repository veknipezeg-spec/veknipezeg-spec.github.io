How to Use This Tool

Universal Video Merger is a free, privacy-first tool that combines multiple video files into a single output. Because everything runs inside your browser using WebAssembly, your files are never uploaded to any server — they stay on your device the entire time.

Step 1: Drop or select the video files you want to merge.

Step 2: Reorder them using the up/down arrows if needed.

Step 3: Choose your preferred output resolution (from 360p up to 4K).

Step 4: Click Merge All Videos and wait for processing.

Step 5: Download the combined MP4 file when finished.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is my data safe? Are my videos uploaded anywhere?

    No. This tool uses FFmpeg.wasm, which runs entirely in your web browser. Your video files never leave your device and are never uploaded to our servers. Once you close or refresh the page, all temporary data is cleared from memory.

    What video formats are supported?

    We support most common formats including MP4, MKV, AVI, MOV, WebM, FLV, WMV, M4V, MPG, MPEG, 3GP, TS, VOB, MTS, and M2TS. Videos with different codecs or resolutions are automatically normalized to match the chosen output settings before merging.

    How long does merging take?

    Processing time depends on the number of files, their length, and the output resolution. A small 720p video typically takes 30-60 seconds. Larger files or 4K output can take several minutes. The first load of the FFmpeg engine may also take up to a minute.

    Is there a file size or length limit?

    There is no hard-coded limit, but since processing happens in your browser, very large files (over 1.5 GB each) may be slow or run into memory constraints depending on your device. For best results, use modern browsers like Chrome, Edge, or Firefox on a desktop or laptop.

    Can I merge videos with different resolutions?

    Yes. Before concatenating, every video is normalized to your chosen output resolution, frame rate, and audio format. This ensures smooth playback of the final merged file without black screens or audio sync issues.

    What happens if a video has no audio track?

    The tool automatically detects silent videos and injects a silent audio track so that concatenation works correctly. This prevents common FFmpeg concat errors that occur when mixing videos with and without audio.

    Do I need to install anything?

    No. The tool is completely web-based. No software, browser extension, or account is required. Just open this page, drop your files, and merge.

    Why Use a Video Merger?

    Combining multiple clips into a single video is a common need for content creators, educators, podcasters, and anyone editing footage from smartphones, cameras, or screen recorders. Merging is often needed to create compilations, combine lecture segments, stitch together travel vlogs, or prepare footage for upload to YouTube, Instagram, or TikTok.

    Unlike traditional desktop software, this tool requires no installation, leaves no temporary files on your computer, and works equally well on Windows, macOS, Linux, and Chromebooks.

    Common Support Topics

    Merging fails on large files: Try reducing the output resolution or merging fewer files at once. Browser memory is limited.

    Tool doesn't load on mobile: Use a recent version of Chrome, Firefox, or Safari. Some older mobile browsers lack WebAssembly support.

    Output video has no audio: Ensure all source videos have an audio track, or the tool will add silence automatically.
